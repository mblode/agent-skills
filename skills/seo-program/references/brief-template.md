# Content Brief Template

Copy this for step 4. Keep it short: a brief is a set of decisions, not a draft.

```markdown
# Brief: <non-brand topic, not the company name>

Date: <YYYY-MM-DD>  ·  Status: Request

## Why this page exists

Audience: <who, specifically>
Gap: <what they cannot find today>
Job of the page: <decide | compare | implement | understand>

## SEO decisions

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Primary keyword | <with modifier> |
| Search volume | <number> (<source>, <scope>, <window>) |
| Exact prompt volume | <number or "No data"> (<source>, <window>) |
| Secondary keywords | <term> (<volume>), <term> (<volume>) |
| Title | <non-brand primary keyword leads> |
| H1 | <matches the title in intent> |
| Meta description | <one sentence, written not generated> |
| Canonical | <URL> |

## North star

Tone: <how it should read>
The reader should leave knowing: <one sentence>

## Ideas to land

- <idea>
- <idea>
- <idea>

Three to five. Not an outline: the writer decides the order and the section breaks.

## Shape

Problem → evidence → questions → how to do it → short close.

## Questions worth answering

Evaluator questions first, product-specific after. Mark anything already covered elsewhere.

1. <question a person would type>
2. <question a person would type>

## Sources the writer can cite

- <public URL>
- <public URL>

Public product pages, public docs, and published research only.
```

## What stays out

- Internal feature lists. The writer needs the reader's problem, not the roadmap.
- A section-by-section outline. If every heading is specified, the brief is a draft and the writer is a typist.
- Private wiki, staging, or internal-doc URLs. They cannot be cited and they leak.
- Invented statistics, quotes, or product behaviour. Anything the writer cannot verify will either be cut late or shipped wrong.

## Versioning

One brief, one file, one date. A revision is the next brief, not an edit over the last one. The pair of files is the record of what changed between the two asks.
