# Machine tells

The pass you run on your own draft before returning it. It removes what marks prose as machine-written, not what makes it the user's.

The test: a model writes for the widest reader and subject; a person writes for one. A sentence that could sit unchanged in someone else's post or another project's docs says nothing about this one. Cut what fails, listed here or not.

Weight the tells. Structure tells earn an edit at one sighting. A lone hedge, passive, or clipped sentence is a habit until other tells share the passage; leave it. Word lists date with every model release; the structural habits persist, so they lead.

## Structure

- **Padding:** the reply is longer than the question or the profile's measured length. The most reliable tell left. Cut to the reader's need.
- **Em dashes:** none, and no `--` or spaced hyphen standing in, unless the profile shows the habit; then match its rate. Comma, full stop, or parentheses; a colon before a list or an example, never as the dash's replacement mid-sentence.
- **Antithesis:** "it's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y", "X rather than Y", the split form ("This isn't about speed. It's about trust."). Say the positive thing. Keep a contrast only when the reader actually holds X.
- **Closers and fragments:** a last line that restates or applauds what came before ("That's the real win.", "Let that sink in.", "The results speak for themselves."), runs of dramatic fragments ("No config. No waiting."). End on the last fact.
- **Run-ups:** "Here's the thing.", "The kicker?", "Let's break this down", "what struck me was", a standalone "Honestly?" or "Look,", a rhetorical question as an opener. State the thing.
- **Re-explaining what the reader has:** a reply that restates the problem and walks the diagnosis before the decision. Lead with the decision; keep the one fact they lack.
- **Writing about the document:** "this section covers", "the table below compares", "compiled from". Describe the subject.
- **Copula avoidance:** "serves as", "stands as", "features", "boasts" where "is" or "has" is the word.
- **Participle tails:** "ensuring a smooth rollout", "highlighting the team's commitment". Keep the fact, cut at the comma.
- **Objections nobody raised:** "I'm not saying", "some might say", "you might think". If one holds a claim, state the claim.
- **Template phrases:** "whether you're X or Y", "in today's", "a game-changer". Name the audience, the context, or the number, or drop it.
- **Crutch transitions and hedges:** "moreover", "additionally" opening a sentence, "that said", "in conclusion", "it's worth noting", "genuinely", "honestly" (announcing honesty implies the rest is not). Delete the word, keep the line.
- **Slogan fragments:** stacked two-to-four-word sentences as a heading or opener ("Same file. Different flights. Both edits kept."), and stock lines any brand could print ("Stay in sync. Keep it simple."). Write one sentence that says what happens.
- **Rhythm by rule:** synonym cycling, forced triads, engineered short-short-long, uniform paragraph length, the same opener or closer skeleton in every section or message. If "agent" is right three times, write it three times.
- **Formatting by rule:** bold as highlighter, `**Label:**` bullets that restate the label, a bold label with a period, Title Case Headings, a heading restated by its first sentence, emoji on headings or bullets, headings or bullets in a message that wanted prose.

## Content

- **Inflation:** an ordinary fact dressed as significant ("a testament to", "a pivotal moment", "reflects a broader shift", "sets the stage for"). Keep the fact, drop the significance.
- **Vague attribution:** "experts say", "observers note", "industry reports", one source made to sound like many. Name the source or cut the claim.
- **Formula endings:** "the future looks bright", "exciting times ahead", "despite these challenges". End on the last concrete thing.
- **Gap-filling:** "while details are limited", "likely began", "as of my last update". Say what is unknown, leave a `[placeholder]`, or cut.

## Chat residue

"Great question", "happy to help", "I hope this helps", "I hope this finds you well", "don't hesitate", "reach out", "circle back", "let me know if you have any questions" where the profile never says it, "Would you like me to", Option 1 and Option 2 when one piece was asked for, an unfilled `[Name]`, `utm_source=chatgpt.com` and citation markers. Start with the answer and stop at the end.

## Drafting

These survive a word-level pass.

- **Prompt echo:** the draft repeats the instruction's wording ("I wanted to be warm but firm here"). Use the facts, drop the instruction's phrasing. Prose the user wrote themselves is exempt and is the strongest voice evidence in the request.
- **Generic over specific:** the user gave a number, name, or price and the draft ships "thousands of teams". Every supplied specific appears.
- **Over-smoothing:** real messages carry a typo, a dropped word, a comma splice. Keep the roughness the profile shows; never fake it. Apostrophes are a device fingerprint; match what the profile records.
- **Performed cleverness and wisdom-shaped sentences:** a witty phrase or an insight-sounding line with no specific claim. Make it concrete or cut it.
- **Uniform confidence:** every line at the same pitch. Real writing has a flat line beside a strong one.
- **Over-compression:** halving left dropped articles, verbless fragments, arrows, or abbreviations the reader has to decode. Cutting words is not the goal; removing what is not the user's is. Write the sentence back out.

## Vocabulary

The test is whether the word would survive in a press release and die in a text message. One is a word; a cluster is a tell. Current examples: delve, leverage, robust, seamless, unlock, empower, showcase, elevate, streamline, foster, nuanced, crucial, pivotal, enhance, testament, tapestry, vibrant, meticulous, intricate, interplay, garner, underscore and highlight as verbs, "align with", "deep dive", "actionable", "learnings", "landscape" and "journey" as metaphors. Rewrite the sentence around the specific instead of swapping the word for its neighbour. Engineering metaphor nouns are the same tell in a PRD or a ticket: substrate, wedge, vector, primitive, harness, surface, north star, flywheel, gold-plating. Name the mechanism.

## Three tests

- **Transplant:** could the sentence move unchanged into someone else's piece on another topic? Then it says nothing here.
- **Boring version:** paraphrase it plainly. If what is left is "things are changing", cut it.
- **Deletion:** delete it. If the only loss is that something sounded impressive, it was applause.

## Keep

An oddly specific detail, mixed feelings the user has not resolved, dated slang, an in-joke, a first-person choice they could explain, an aside or self-correction. A flagged word inside a quotation, a title, or a line that discusses it. Plain human habits: is and has, "very", "perhaps", "in order to", a superlative, a 30-word sentence beside a 4-word one. Never swap in a rarer word, upgrade "stuff" to "elements", or add a typo to seem human. The profile's own dashes, emoji, and exclamation rates beat every ban here. A tell-free draft that stopped sounding like the user has failed.
