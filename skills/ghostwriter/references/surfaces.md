# Surfaces

Shape defaults where the profile is silent. The profile always wins; emoji, apostrophes, and sign-offs come from it, never from here.

**Chat (Slack, WhatsApp, LinkedIn message).** One line unless the question was long; answer in the shape of the question. A link beats a description. Bursts of short messages when the profile shows them. No greeting mid-thread, no sign-off. An ask names the thing and the time; a decline gives the real reason once.

**Email.** The point in the first sentence; the reply shorter than what it answers. Subject is a plain label, never a sentence. One paragraph per point. Mid-thread the greeting and sign-off drop away.

**LinkedIn post.** One specific thing that happened, one number, at most one lesson stated plainly and never labelled. Three to six short blocks. Named credit. No hashtags. A long draft reads as ghostwritten: cut the weakest block.

**Blog post, essay.** One claim, three or four episodes or numbers that carry it, a close that steps sideways instead of summarising. Few headings, all plain noun phrases. No lists or tables unless the items cannot be narrated. Keep every real number and name; strip every claim with nothing behind it.

**Linear issue, comment.** The gap, then "so" plus the concrete cost; a gap with no cost is not finished. Identifiers, counts, and file:line do the arguing; quote the offending string. At most one heading. Scope fences stated flat. A comment adds one fact or one decision and stops.

**GitHub PR description.** What changed and why in the first line, then how to verify, then what is out of scope. Written like a developer posting in a channel, not a summary of the diff. Opening the PR itself is `pr-creator`.

**GitHub review comment.** The line, the problem, the cost, then the fix proposed once. A nit says it is a nit. No praise padding around a finding.

**PRD.** Problem, who has it and how often, what changes for them, how we will know (a measurable), out of scope, open questions. Drop a section the document does not need rather than fill it. Decisions stated as decisions; open questions left open. Halve it and check the measurable and the scope fences survived.

**Slides.** One idea per slide, the number or claim as the headline, a few words under it, never a paragraph. Deck structure and visuals are `presentation-creator`.

**Talk script.** Spoken register: short sentences, contractions, the story in the order it happened, written to be read aloud. Mark slide changes. No "in this talk I will"; start with the first thing that happened.
