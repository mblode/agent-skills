# Transactional and System Emails

Read when drafting or editing account, payment, recovery, receipt, security, or notification email copy. Deliver the requested copy, not a campaign or an email-service implementation.

## Ground the event

Identify the trigger, recipient, affected account or object, current state, next action, and any expiry or consequence. Use supplied behavior and approved product sources. Do not invent retry schedules, access end dates, refund promises, security conclusions, support channels, or URLs.

Draft around missing noncritical details with clearly marked placeholders. If an unknown determines what happened or what the recipient must do, ask that specific question or label a conditional draft; never present the assumption as a send-ready fact.

## Reuse the right precedent

Find the nearest approved example by purpose and stakes. Match its useful structure and terminology, while checking its claims against current product behavior and current guidance. A shipped example can be outdated. Explain any material correction, especially a changed consequence or expiry, instead of preserving it merely because it shipped.

Use calm, specific wording for recovery and security; proportionate reassurance for payment or account changes. Brand personality remains present through language, but the event and the recipient's next step lead. Keep promotional additions out of a functional message unless the task explicitly calls for mixed content; flag that change of purpose for the owner's review rather than deciding its legal classification.

## Deliver the email's parts

For a full draft, label the parts the implementation needs:

- **Subject:** identifies the event or action accurately.
- **Preheader:** adds useful detail rather than repeating the subject. Essential facts also appear in the body because previews may be hidden.
- **Heading:** or the intentional alternative, such as a one-time code in that slot.
- **Body:** what happened, which account or object it concerns, what happens next, and what the reader can do.
- **Action:** label and destination, if action is needed. A receipt or confirmation may need none. Distinguish secondary help from the primary task.

A request for a subject line or point edit returns only that scope. Do not turn every email into a four-section rewrite.

Links should name their action or destination without depending on screen position. Preserve exact variable names, URLs, amounts, dates, and durations unless a verified contradiction requires correction; state that correction. Missing values stay placeholders. Explain suspicious or unexpected activity only as supported by the actual flow, without manufacturing an incident or an unsupported promise of safety.

## Check copy and handoff separately

For a full draft, check that the subject and preview agree with the body, the event and account are clear, the action matches the destination, and variables survive intact. For a point edit, check only the changed text and relevant context.

When a build or send handoff is requested, distinguish copy checks completed from rendering, link, sender, localization, and real-inbox checks still needed. Do not claim that a draft was tested in an inbox. If an implementation is in scope, use the project's actual tests and delivery workflow.

Keep the project's supplied footer, preference controls, and delivery policies. Do not infer a universal unsubscribe rule from the label "transactional" or borrow another company's legal approvals. Writing the email does not authorize sending it.
