# LinkedIn voice (demo persona)

Fictional demo persona: "Sam", a Melbourne-based senior engineer and indie builder. This file ships so the skill works out of the box. To make it yours, create `~/.config/tone-of-voice/linkedin.md` from this structure and replace the rules and excerpts with your own (see `references/refreshing.md`). Everything below is invented, not real posts.

## Register

Warm, confident, generous, and a bit punchy. First-person builder POV with concrete numbers, sincere gushing about people, and a light CTA. The single biggest tell that a draft is not Sam: flat, wistful, emoji-less, over-balanced sentences.

## Posts

Structure of a strong post:

1. **Punchy hook line.** Personal, mildly contrarian, or a mini-dialogue. Often ends with "!" or "?". ("Our best onboarding change? Asking one fewer question." / "I shipped our most-used feature because I got annoyed with my own app 🤦")
2. **Short story or specifics with numbers.** "$0", "one weekend", "6am before the kids woke up", "900 stars", "40 people", "99.9%".
3. **One distilled lesson, exactly one.** Often opens with "Turns out..." or inverts the question. Stated directly, never labelled ("The lesson for me:", "My takeaway:" are not Sam). If a draft has two lesson lines, cut one.
4. **Light CTA and/or credited names.** "Check it out:", "Give it a try:", "Slides here if you're keen:". Names tagged in full, often clustered on their own line at the end.

Formatting rules:

- 3-6 short blocks total, blank line between each. Scannable, feed-friendly. Longer drafts read as ghostwritten; cut the weakest block.
- Humour is self-deprecating and earnest ("I got annoyed with my own app 🤦"), never wry or sardonic.
- One or two emoji per post, usually at the end of a line: 🙌 🚀 💪 ❤️ 😊 ☝️ 🎉 🤦 👉. Zero emoji = not Sam.
- Genuine enthusiasm about people: "couldn't ask for a better team ❤️", "Legends", "So proud".
- Bullet lists with "•" or numbered lists for product specifics work well.
- A closing question to the audience is on-brand: "What's one product you love for keeping it simple?"
- No hashtags.

## DMs

Short, warm, exclamatory, and useful. Four recurring shapes:

- **Meetup follow-up**: "Hey, great to meet you last night!" + a specific compliment + a link or next step.
- **Intro between two people**: name both, one line of context each, "I'll let you both take it from here", sign "Thanks, Sam" (the only LinkedIn surface where Sam signs off).
- **Logistics**: identical to email/WhatsApp scheduling ("Hey, do you want to grab a coffee tomorrow?" / "Perfect, does 12 work?").
- **Pure celebration**: unfiltered hype ("Ayyyyy!!! Congrats!!").

DMs are answered helpfully and concretely, naming the right person to talk to and linking their profile.

## Comments

Two modes:

- **Hype/gratitude** (most common): 1-2 sentences, doubled exclamation marks, one emoji. "Congrats!!" + a specific reason. "Thank you so much for organising! Had a blast and keen for the next one 🙌". When replying to congratulations on an own post: "[Name] thank you!! Keen to see what you build 😊".
- **Substantive answer**: when someone asks a real question, a real answer with specifics and numbers, same as the Slack channel voice, sometimes 2-3 short paragraphs.

## Anti-patterns

- Em dashes anywhere (automatic fail).
- Manicured, perfectly balanced sentences with a tacked-on verdict ("That's a good deal.").
- Asserting value without delivering it ("taught me a lot", "done it at scale") instead of a concrete detail or number.
- No emoji, no warmth, no named credit.
- Hashtags, "Agree?", "Let that sink in", listicle-bait hooks.
- Two lesson lines in one post, or a labelled lesson ("The lesson:", "My takeaway:").
- Wry, sardonic, or world-weary asides; the energy is sincere.
- Flat humility or false modesty; wins are celebrated plainly and everyone gets credited.

## Excerpts

**Post: hook, specifics, lesson, question**
> Our best onboarding change? Asking one fewer question.

> New Tidepool users used to hit a 5-field setup screen and half of them bailed there.

> So we cut it to one:
> • You tell us one habit
> • We pull the rest from your calendar
> • You tweak later if you want

> Setup time dropped from ~90 seconds to under 15, and completion went from 48% to 81%

> Turns out people don't want to configure, they want to start 🙌

> What's one product you love for keeping it simple?

**Post: self-deprecating origin story with numbers**
> I shipped our most-used feature because I got annoyed with my own app 🤦

> After staring at an empty dashboard one too many times, I hacked together a "sample week" button at 6am before the kids woke up

> It's now the first thing 3 in 4 new users tap

> Turns out dogfooding isn't just good practice, it's how you find the thing your users hit on day one

**Post: event recap with generous credit**
> Great night at the Melbourne indie hackers meetup!

> Forty-odd builders packed into [Venue]. [Name] walked through going from side project to $10k MRR, then quickfire demos from people actually shipping

> Thanks [Name] and [Community] for hosting. Legends. Good to finally meet [Name] in person too 🙌

**Post: team appreciation**
> One year ago today I teamed up with [Name] and [Name] to build Tidepool!

> I write the code. [Name] talks to every single user and somehow remembers all their names.

> Couldn't ask for better people to build with ❤️

**Post: distilled lesson from a talk**
> Spoke to a room of new founders this week about shipping fast with a tiny team.

> Invert the question. Don't ask how to ship faster, ask what's slowing you down. Then delete it.

> Slides here if you're keen: [link]

> Thanks [Name] and [Name] for having me 😊

**DM: meetup follow-up**
> Hey, great to meet you last night! [Product] looks epic! Congrats on landing [Name] as your first big customer 💪

**DM: intro between two people**
> Hi [Name], [Name], connecting you two.

>
> I was thinking about your work on [Project], it's exactly the kind of thing they're after
>
> I'll let you both take it from here
>
> Thanks,
> Sam

**DM: polite decline**
> Hey [Name], can't make it unfortunately! But it looks like a great event!

**Comments: hype and gratitude**
> Love this!! Congrats on getting it live 🎉🔥

> Huge congrats [Name]!! [Company]'s lucky to have you!

> Thank you so much for organising! Had a blast and keen for the next one 🙌

> [Name] thank you!! Keen to hear what you think of it

**Comment: substantive answer with specifics**
> [Name] we measured the time from sign-up to a user actually logging their first habit.

> So we front-loaded one question (what habit) and used their calendar to pre-fill the rest. By the time they hit the dashboard it was already populated, so there was almost nothing to set up.

> The retention difference in week one was bigger than anything else we tried that quarter
